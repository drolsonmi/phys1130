# Solutions to Meteorology Exam Study Guide: Chapters 12–16
## Practice Problems

*Try these on your own first, then check your work with the answer key below.*

1. List the stages in the life cycle of a mid-latitude cyclone according to the Norwegian cyclone model.
2. A surface low has strong convergence near the ground, but even stronger divergence aloft. Will the low strengthen or weaken? Explain.
3. Relative to an upper-level trough, where is the best location for a surface low to develop? Relative to a jet streak in the NH?
4. A storm's central pressure falls from 1000 mb to 972 mb in 24 hours. Does this qualify as bombogenesis (assume no latitude adjustment)?
5. A forecaster is 70% confident that rain will fall, and expects it to cover 60% of the area. What is the PoP?
6. Which forecasting method would work best for (a) a forecast 30 days ahead, (b) the next 30 minutes as a line of storms approaches, (c) tomorrow during a long stretch of identical sunny days?
7. Explain the difference between a Tornado Watch and a Tornado Warning. Who issues each?
8. An ensemble's members show very different storm tracks. What does that tell a forecaster?
9. Why do ordinary-cell thunderstorms usually die out within an hour, while supercells can last for hours?
10. Describe what happens to the weather when a gust front passes, and explain how gust fronts keep multicell storms going.
11. You see a lightning flash and hear thunder 15 seconds later. How far away is the strike? Is it safe to stay outside?
12. Put these in order for a cloud-to-ground lightning strike: return stroke, streamer, stepped leader, dart leader.
13. How do lightning rods protect a building? Do they attract lightning?
14. A tornado has rotational winds of 120 mph and moves northeast at 35 mph. What are the approximate wind speeds on its right and left sides?
15. Why is a dust devil not considered a tornado, while a landspout is?
16. The EF Scale is based on damage. Give one advantage and one limitation of this approach.
17. List the conditions needed for a tropical cyclone to form, and explain why hurricanes don't form at the equator.
18. A storm has maximum sustained winds of 115 mph. What is its classification? Is it a major hurricane?
19. A hurricane is moving north toward a coastline that runs east–west. Where would you expect the highest storm surge, and why?
20. A town is located 50 miles outside of the forecast cone. Can it ignore the hurricane? Explain.

---

## Answer Key

1. Stationary front → frontal wave (incipient cyclone) → open wave (warm and cold fronts with a warm sector) → mature stage / beginning of occlusion → occluded stage → dissipation.
2. It will **strengthen**. More air is removed aloft than flows in at the surface, so the mass of the air column decreases and the **surface pressure falls**.
3. **Downstream (east) of the upper-level trough**, where there is divergence aloft. For a jet streak, beneath the **left exit** or **right entrance** region.
4. The drop is 28 mb in 24 hours, which is **more than 24 mb**, so **yes**, it qualifies.
5. PoP = 0.70 × 0.60 = **0.42, or about 40%**.
6. (a) **Climatology**; (b) **Trend** (or nowcasting); (c) **Persistence**.
7. A **watch** means conditions are **favorable** for tornadoes (be prepared); it is issued by the **Storm Prediction Center** for large areas. A **warning** means a tornado is **occurring or imminent**, detected by radar or spotters (take shelter now); it is issued by the local **NWS Weather Forecast Office**.
8. Large **spread** means **low confidence**: small differences in the starting conditions lead to very different outcomes, so the forecast is uncertain.
9. Ordinary cells form with **weak wind shear**, so the downdraft falls into the updraft and cuts off the supply of warm, moist air. Supercells form with **strong shear**, which keeps the updraft and downdraft **separated** and makes the updraft rotate, allowing them to persist.
10. A sudden **wind shift** and **strong gusts**, a sharp **temperature drop**, and a **pressure rise**. The gust front acts like a mini cold front, **lifting warm, moist air** ahead of it to form **new cells**.
11. 15 ÷ 5 = **3 miles**. **No**: if you can hear thunder, you are within striking distance; go indoors.
12. **Stepped leader → streamer → return stroke → dart leader** (followed by additional return strokes).
13. They **intercept** a strike that would hit the building anyway and carry the current safely **into the ground** through conductors and grounding rods. They **do not attract** lightning from long distances.
14. Right side: 120 + 35 = **155 mph**; left side: 120 − 35 = **85 mph**.
15. A dust devil forms from **intense surface heating** under **clear skies** and is **not connected to a cloud**. A landspout is attached to a **cumuliform cloud**/developing thunderstorm, forming as rotation along a boundary is stretched by the storm's updraft, so it meets the definition of a tornado.
16. **Advantage:** Wind speeds inside tornadoes are rarely measured, but damage can be surveyed for almost every tornado, giving a consistent rating system. **Limitation:** A tornado that hits nothing (or only weak structures) can't be rated accurately, so its true intensity may be underestimated; ratings also depend on construction quality and the surveyor's judgment.
17. **Warm ocean water** (≥26.5 °C/80 °F to ~50 m deep), a **pre-existing disturbance** (low pressure trigger), **weak vertical wind shear**, **moist, unstable air**, and enough **Coriolis force**. At the equator, the Coriolis force is **zero**, so the converging winds cannot begin to rotate.
18. A **Category 3** hurricane (111–129 mph). **Yes**, it is a **major hurricane**.
19. On the **east side** of the landfall point (the **right-front quadrant**). There, the counterclockwise winds blow **onshore** and in the **same direction** as the storm's motion, so wind speed and forward speed add, pushing the most water toward the coast.
20. **No.** The cone shows only the likely path of the **center**, which stays inside the cone only about two-thirds of the time. Wind, surge, heavy rain, and tornadoes often extend well **outside** the cone. The town should follow its local watches and warnings.
