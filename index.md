# Welcome to PHYS 1130: Introduction to Meteorology

Welcome to Introduction to Meteorology, a physics-based exploration of Earth's atmosphere and the processes that produce weather and climate. Throughout this course, we will examine how fundamental principles of physics govern atmospheric behavior, from the formation of clouds and storms to global climate patterns.

Meteorology is much more than forecasting the weather. It is the study of energy, motion, pressure, temperature, and the interactions between the atmosphere, oceans, and land surfaces. In this course, you will learn how physical laws help us understand and predict atmospheric phenomena that affect our daily lives.


## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, you should be able to:

- Explain the structure, composition, and origin of Earth's atmosphere.
- Apply basic physics principles to atmospheric processes.
- Understand how energy transfer drives weather and climate.
- Analyze atmospheric motion and circulation patterns.
- Interpret weather maps, observations, and forecasts.
- Explain the development of severe weather events.
- Evaluate the factors that influence Earth's climate system.


## Course Organization

The course is organized into four major segments that build from foundational concepts to large-scale atmospheric and climate processes.


### 1. Formation of the Atmosphere

We begin by examining the origin, composition, and structure of Earth's atmosphere. Topics include:

- Atmospheric composition and layers
- Solar radiation and Earth's energy budget
- Temperature, pressure, and density relationships
- Water vapor and the hydrologic cycle
- Cloud formation and atmospheric stability

Throughout this segment, we will use principles from thermodynamics and energy transfer to understand how the atmosphere developed and how it functions today.


### 2. Motions of the Atmosphere

Next, we investigate why air moves and how atmospheric circulation develops. Topics include:

- Forces acting on the atmosphere
- Pressure gradients and wind formation
- Coriolis effect and Earth's rotation
- Forms of Precipitation
- Global circulation patterns
- Air masses and fronts

Physics provides the framework for understanding motion, forces, and fluid behavior within the atmosphere.


### 3. Severe Weather and Forecasting

In this segment, we apply atmospheric principles to the study of weather systems and forecasting. Topics include:

- Mid-latitude cyclones and weather systems
- Forecast models and prediction methods
- Thunderstorms and lightning
- Tornadoes 
- Hurricanes

Students will learn how meteorologists use observations, physical laws, and computer models to predict weather and communicate risk.


### 4. Climate

The final segment broadens our perspective from short-term weather to long-term atmospheric behavior. Topics include:

- Climate controls and feedbacks
- Climate classification systems
- Global climate change
- Human influences on climate
- Pollution

This section emphasizes the physical processes that regulate Earth's climate system and shape environmental conditions across the globe.


## Physics in Meteorology

As a physics course, PHYS 1130 emphasizes the scientific principles that drive atmospheric processes. Rather than simply describing weather events, we will explore *why* they occur by applying concepts from:

- Mechanics
- Thermodynamics
- Fluid dynamics
- Radiation and energy transfer
- Waves and atmospheric motion

These concepts allow us to connect physical laws to real-world weather and climate phenomena.


## Getting Started

Success in this course comes from actively engaging with the material, asking questions, and connecting atmospheric observations to the physical principles discussed in class. By the end of the semester, you will have a deeper understanding of how the atmosphere works and how physics helps explain the weather and climate around us.
